2 by 2 wrist counter: repair guide

One button moves between four positions set in a square, 14 mm apart. It is worn on the wrist. There are two tops: one for a watch strap on spring bars, one for a strap threaded through it.

Draft. Written from the design files and the designer's answers. No step on this page has been followed on a printed counter. The marks are explained on the front page.

Tools needed

  • For the printed parts: a 3D printer, or someone with one. The shells are STEP files in down-indicator-watch/, and the button is down-counter-umpire/button.step. PrusaSlicer, OrcaSlicer, and Bambu Studio open STEP files directly. Print material and settings are Not recorded.
  • For opening and closing: Not recorded. We do not yet know whether the shells come apart by hand or need a tool.
  • For the watch-strap top: whatever you use to change a watch strap on spring bars. Which tool suits these horns is Not checked on a printed unit.
  • A tray, and a phone for photos.

How to open it

Take the strap off first; see strap. Then, what the files show: From the files

  • The bottom shell sits inside the top shell's skirt. The opening it was pressed in through is about 0.18 mm a side narrower than the bottom shell, so the skirt grips it.
  • The bottom shell stands 1.1 mm proud of the skirt all the way round. That edge is the only part of it you can reach.
  • The holes in the bottom shell are closed at the back, over a 2.0 mm floor. There is nothing to push through from behind.

So the only way apart is to bring the bottom shell straight back out of the skirt, the way it went in.

Not checked on a printed unit Nobody has opened a pressed-together counter and recorded how: by hand or with a tool, how much effort it takes, and whether the shells still hold after they go back together. Until that is checked we cannot give a safe method. Do not lever against the skirt wall; it is 2.0 mm thick.

How to replace each part

Every replacement starts with the counter open and ends with closing it.

Button

  1. With the bottom shell out, lift the button out of the pocket on the underside of the top shell. It cannot come out through the face. From the files
  2. Move the pin and magnets from the old button to the new one, the same way round. How they are held in the button is Not recorded.
  3. Put the new button into the pocket, sleeve through the opening. From the files

Pin and magnets

  1. Before you take any magnet out, photograph which holes hold one, and mark the face that points up on each. A magnet put back upside down will push where it should pull.
  2. Replacement magnets are 6 mm × 2 mm discs. From the designer On paper one fits the holes and the button's hole with 0.015 mm to spare a side, so expect a push fit. From the files
  3. Put each new magnet where the old one was, the same face up.
Not recorded How many magnets there are, where they sit, which way they face, what the pin is, and whether the magnets are pressed in or glued. If a magnet does not come out easily, stop: it may be glued, and we do not know with what.

Top shell

  1. Take the button out of the old top and put it into the pocket of the new one. From the files
  2. Close it with the old bottom shell.

Either top fits the same bottom shell and button, so this is also how to change from one kind of strap to the other. From the files

Bottom shell

  1. Move the magnets from the old bottom shell to the same holes in the new one, the same face up. See pin and magnets.
  2. Close it with the old top shell.

Strap

The strap comes off without opening the counter.

  • Watch-strap top. The strap sits on two spring bars between the horns. Each bar's tips sit in the 1.1 mm cross holes. Compress a bar to free it, slide the new 22 mm strap on, and spring the tips back into the holes. From the files Not checked on a printed unit
  • Elastic-strap top. No spring bars. The strap passes over the bar at each end and through the opening. Pull the old strap out and thread the new one the same way. A 24 mm strap fills the opening with no room to spare. From the files The designer reports that this tight fit keeps the counter from sliding. From the designer

Which strap we supply with each top, and its length, is Not recorded. Copy the one you have.

Cord

None. This counter has no cord.

How to close it

  1. Lay the top shell face down.
  2. Put the button in first, into the pocket, sleeve through the opening. Once the shells are together the button cannot be added. From the files
  3. Turn the bottom shell so its holes face the button. It is square and goes in any of four ways round. From the files Before you opened it you photographed which holes hold magnets; put it back the same way round, because whether the way round matters is Not recorded.
  4. Press the bottom shell into the skirt until it seats against the underside of the plate. It then stands 1.1 mm proud of the skirt; the counter is 8.2 mm thick, and 10.1 mm over the button. From the files How much force that takes, and whether a tool is needed, is Not checked on a printed unit.
  5. Check: the button moves freely in the square opening and holds at each of the four positions. How firmly it should hold is Not recorded.
  6. Fit the strap.

What damage looks like, and what not to force

No damaged counter has been examined and written up, so what a failed part looks like on this counter is Not recorded. These are the thinnest places in the design, and so the places to look at and to keep tools away from: From the files

PlaceSizeDo not
Skirt wall of the top shell2.0 mmLever against it or squeeze it to free the bottom shell.
Button flange1.0 mm thickPull the button out through the face. It only comes out from underneath.
Horns of the watch-strap top6 mm wide, with a 1.1 mm hole through eachSpread the horns to get a spring bar in or out. Compress the bar instead.
End bars of the elastic-strap topA rounded bar across a 24 mm openingForce a strap wider than 24 mm through, or lever on the bar.
Floor under each magnet hole2.0 mmDrive a magnet out from the back, or drill it.

Stop and replace the part if you find a crack, a piece that stays bent, or a shell that no longer grips the other. If the button slides but no longer holds at a position, look at the pin and magnets first.
