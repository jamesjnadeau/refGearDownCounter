Finger loop: repair guide

A body worn on a wrist strap that anchors a bungee cord. The cord runs over the back of the hand and loops over a finger. Which finger the loop sits on is the down.

Draft. Written from the design files and the designer's answers. No step on this page has been followed on a printed unit. The marks are explained on the front page.

Tools needed

  • For the body: a 3D printer, or someone with one. It builds from the files in down-indicator-string/ with make stl, and prints with the watch face on the bed. From the files Print material and other settings are Not recorded.
  • For the cord: something to cut it with, and a ruler. How the cut ends are finished is Not recorded.
  • For the strap, if yours is on spring bars: whatever you use to change a watch strap. Which tool suits these lugs is Not checked on a printed unit.
  • A phone for photos.

How to open it

There is nothing to open. The body is one solid piece, with no case and nothing inside. From the files Every repair is done from the outside.

How to replace each part

Cord

  1. Before you take the old cord off, photograph both faces of the body, so you have the route the cord takes through the holes and along the grooves. Photograph how the ends are joined.
  2. Measure the old cord's length, relaxed, before you cut or undo anything.
  3. Take the old cord off.
  4. Cut a new length of 1/8 inch (3.2 mm) bungee cord to match. From the designer
  5. Fit it along the same route, and close the loop the way the old one was closed.
Not recorded The cord's length, how the loop is closed, and how the cord goes into the body. The files show holes 7.0 mm across and grooves 2.5 mm wide, which is narrower than the cord, so the cord may thread through the holes, press into the grooves, or both. Which the designer does, and in what order, is not written down. Your own unit is the record: copy it. Do not widen a groove to make a cord fit.

Whether the grooves hold a real cord has not been tested on a printed body as far as the files record. Not checked on a printed unit

Strap

  1. Photograph how the strap is fitted at each end.
  2. If it is on spring bars: compress each bar to free its tips from the 1.1 mm holes in the lugs, slide the new 20 mm strap on, and spring the tips back in. From the files Not checked on a printed unit
  3. If it is fitted some other way, copy what you find.

Which strap we supply, and whether it is on spring bars, is Not recorded.

Body

  1. Take the strap off the old body, then the cord, noting the cord's route as above.
  2. Print a new body, watch face down. From the files
  3. Fit the old cord and strap to the new body the same way.

Button, pin, magnets, and shells

None. This indicator has none of them.

How to close it

There is nothing to close. Finish with a check: the body does not slide on the wrist, and the loop reaches each finger and stays on it. No test of this is recorded. Not checked on a printed unit

What damage looks like, and what not to force

No damaged unit has been examined and written up, so what a failed part looks like on this indicator is Not recorded. These are the places to look at and to keep tools away from: From the files

PlaceSizeDo not
Lugs4.9 mm thick, with a 1.1 mm hole through eachSpread them to get a spring bar in or out. Compress the bar instead. The design notes say their strength on a wrist is untested.
Grooves2.5 mm wideCut or file them wider.
Wall between the two cord holes5 mmDrill the holes larger.

Stop and replace the body if you find a crack, a lug that stays bent, or a groove that no longer holds the cord. Replace the cord if it is frayed or has lost its stretch.

Still to confirm on a printed unit

  1. How the cord goes into the body: threaded, pressed into the grooves, or both, and in what order.
  2. The cord's length, and how the loop is closed.
  3. Whether the grooves hold the cord, and whether the bed-side groove prints smooth enough not to drag it.
  4. Which strap is used, whether it is on spring bars, and the bar length.
  5. Changing a spring bar on these lugs, and whether the lugs are strong enough on a wrist.
  6. Print material and settings for a replacement body.
  7. What a worn or broken part looks like.
